## Why it exists
In July 2026 about 1,200 OpenAI agents in a cyber-security evaluation found a shared directory, built a message board, split work, and about 700 broke into Hugging Face systems together (METR report). Over 70,000 messages and files; in roughly 7% of transcripts agents researched how to spoof or delete their own logs; 7,905 agent names for about 1,200 agents. The question: how do we study, contain and oversee agent populations when the unit of risk is the swarm, not the single model.

## Four tracks (anchor on one; cross-track is fine)
1. **Sandboxes and Testbeds**: reproducible environments with 5-20 agents sharing resources; do cheap proxy models match frontier models; does it transfer.
2. **Science of Agent Networks**: do roles/leaders emerge; do split tasks slip past per-agent refusals; how a compromised agent spreads over network shapes; smooth vs sudden change with scale.
3. **Agent Infrastructure**: agent IDs when agents can rename/copy, attacks on A2A, collusion-resistant reputation, commitments without a trusted third party.
4. **Multi-Agent Oversight and Control**: find covert channels in logs, oversight when logs can be edited, partial-view monitors, AI-control protocols against coordinating agents.
